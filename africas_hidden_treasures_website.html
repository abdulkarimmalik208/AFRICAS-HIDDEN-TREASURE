Introduction

West Africa has a long history of trade in gold and salt. The Ghana, Mali and Songhai empires grew around important trade routes and centres in the Sahel. Today, parts of West Africa are also known for significant gold deposits and petroleum resources. Studying them together shows how geography and natural resources have influenced the region across time.

Important distinction: The medieval Ghana Empire was centred mainly in areas of present-day southeastern Mauritania and western Mali. It was not located in the same territory as the modern Republic of Ghana, which adopted the historic name at independence in 1957.

1. The three historic empires

c. 6th–13th centuries

Ghana Empire

Centred around the western Sahel, with its royal centre commonly associated with Koumbi Saleh. Its rulers gained wealth through taxing and protecting trans-Saharan trade, especially gold and salt.

Modern geography: mainly southeastern Mauritania and western Mali.

c. 1235–1600

Mali Empire

Founded by Sundiata Keita. It expanded across a large part of the western Sahel. Mansa Musa became famous for Mali's wealth, while Timbuktu and Djenné developed as important centres of trade and learning.

Modern geography: mainly Mali, with territories at different times extending into neighbouring countries.

c. 15th–16th centuries

Songhai Empire

Grew around Gao and the middle Niger River. Sonni Ali expanded the empire, and Askia Muhammad strengthened its administration, scholarship and trade. It became one of the largest states in West African history.

Modern geography: mainly Mali and parts of neighbouring Sahel countries at its height.

3. Gold and crude oil in present-day Africa

Gold

Gold remains an important resource in the West African region. Ghana, Mali and Burkina Faso are established gold-producing countries. Important gold districts also occur in Mauritania, Guinea, Côte d’Ivoire and Senegal. The Birimian geological belts of West Africa host many gold deposits.

Crude oil and natural gas

Petroleum is found in sedimentary basins, including offshore areas along the Atlantic coast. Ghana has producing offshore fields in the Tano/Cape Three Points Basin. Mauritania and Senegal have developed the Greater Tortue Ahmeyim offshore gas project. Namibia, Kenya, Uganda and Mozambique are among countries with significant hydrocarbon discoveries or development activity, although not every discovery is crude oil and not every discovery is producing.

4. How geologists investigate possible resources

1. Geological mapping

Scientists study rock types, structures and the geological history of an area.

2. Geophysical surveys

Methods such as seismic surveys help image underground rock layers and structures.

3. Geochemical studies

Rock, soil and other samples are analysed for chemical clues that may indicate mineralisation or petroleum systems.

4. Exploration drilling

Where evidence supports a target, licensed specialists drill and analyse samples to test what is underground.

5. Evaluation

Experts estimate the size, quality, technical difficulty and environmental and economic feasibility of a resource.

5. Conclusion

The Ghana, Mali and Songhai empires demonstrate the historic importance of West Africa's trade networks and gold wealth. Modern geological knowledge shows that the region continues to contain valuable mineral resources, while sedimentary basins across Africa have yielded petroleum discoveries and further exploration opportunities. Responsible exploration, environmental protection, good governance and benefits for local communities are essential if these resources are to support sustainable development.
